Assistant Professor (Tenure-Track) · University of the Fraser Valley

Majid BabaeiPH.D.

I pursue accountability by contract for AI agents

I am a tenure-track Assistant Professor in the School of Computing at the University of the Fraser Valley. My research combines software engineering and model-driven engineering (MDE) to make AI agents accountable for their decisions and transparent about their collaborations. I approach this by applying Foundational Contracts1 that allow us to capture human goals, assumptions, and constraints, and connect them to evidence of agent behavior so people can check whether those expectations are met. I hold a Ph.D. in Software Engineering from Queen's University, and my work has received an ACM SIGSOFT Distinguished Paper Award. I advocate for open research software and bring experience from both academia and industry.

§ 01 — Research agenda

Closing the loop between humans and agentic AI

Agentic AI systems plan, delegate, and collaborate, yet they rarely see the higher-level concerns people care about, and people rarely see how agent teams reach their objectives. My research builds abstractions in both directions. Building this loop efficiently and effectively is, I believe, what is missing.

01 · Human → AIabstractions for agents

Connecting agents to higher-level concerns

Safety, correctness, intent, and organizational constraints usually reach an agent as informal prose. I use model-driven engineering (models, contracts, and model transformations) so that agentic systems can be specified against these concerns and checked for them.

02 · AI → Humanabstractions for people

Showing people how agents collaborate

Transcripts, tool logs, and traces are abundant but unreadable at scale. I build abstractions (slices, typed hand-offs, provenance, and knowledge graphs) that explain how agent teams collaborate, where they break, and why an objective was or was not achieved.

Agent Slicing[anonymous for review]HeartnessTAAF · ICSE 2026
03 · The loopwhat is missing

Effective and efficient, together

Specifications without visibility cannot tell us whether they were honored; visibility without specifications gives us nothing to check against. The loop must be effective, with abstractions at the level people reason about yet precise enough to check. It must also be efficient enough to repeat whenever a team, prompt, or model changes.
