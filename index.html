Assistant Professor (Tenure-Track) · University of the Fraser Valley

Majid BabaeiPH.D.

Accountability by contract for AI agents

I am a tenure-track Assistant Professor in the School of Computing at the University of the Fraser Valley. My research combines software engineering and model-driven engineering (MDE) to make the behaviour of AI agents accountable: bounded, attributable, traceable, and correctable, so that the people responsible for agentic systems can actually exercise that responsibility. I approach this through Foundational Contracts1, explicit models that capture human goals, assumptions, and constraints and link them to evidence of what agents actually did. With them, people can check whether those expectations were met and, when they were not, find and undo what went wrong. I hold a Ph.D. in Software Engineering from Queen’s University, and my work has received an ACM SIGSOFT Distinguished Paper Award. I advocate for open research software and bring experience from both academia and industry.

1 By Foundational Contracts I mean explicit, checkable statements, at every boundary in an agentic system, of what each component may assume, what it must guarantee, and how its outputs connect to everything downstream. Accountability here is meant in an engineering sense, not a moral one: responsibility for decisions stays with people, and contracts give them the records and controls to exercise it. § 01 — Research agenda

Accountability by contract for agentic AI

01 · Delegatebounded

Delegating with a contract

Concerns such as safety, intent and organizational rules usually reach an agent as prose. I turn them into foundational contracts: models of what each agent, guardrail and hand-off may assume and must guarantee, composed and checked before the team runs.

SSEVOH-DSL · on-goingTINTSafe CoordinationAutoM2M[anonymous for review]
02 · Reportattributable · traceable

Accounting for what agents did

Transcripts and tool logs are abundant but unreadable at scale. I record hand-offs and provenance while agents work, and abstract them into slices and explanations, so a person can see which agent produced what, from which evidence, and why an objective was or was not met.

Agent Slicing[anonymous for review]TAAF · ICSE 2026
03 · Containcorrectable

Containing what went wrong

When a learned lesson, a skill or a hand-off turns out to be wrong, deleting it is not enough: its effects have already spread into other artifacts and deployments. Recorded lineage finds everything it touched and suspends it everywhere. It is re-admitted only after a fresh check, a bounded number of times, and otherwise explained to the person responsible.

HeartnessSSEVOH-DSL
04 · The contractchecked, not just drawn

One object, checked in both directions

Specifications without evidence cannot tell us whether they were honored; evidence without specifications gives us nothing to check against. A foundational contract holds both. This figure follows its own rule: it is generated from a state-machine model, and on every build a model checker verifies that a person can always be reached and that every violation is contained and ends with a person.
